Kindergarten

On the first morning of kindergarten, Brian was too excited to sit still.

He had a brand new backpack, stiff and squeaky and far too big for him, and he insisted on wearing it the whole way there even though there was almost nothing in it yet. Mum and Dad walked him across the playground at Teck Whye Lane, down the little hill, and around the corner to the kindergarten, where a crowd of other small children was already gathering. The parents did not go inside. They stood out in the corridor and peered in through the big windows, a row of grown-up faces pressed to the glass.

At the door, a teacher with a wide, kind face was welcoming everyone in.

"Good morning! Come in, come in," she said. "My name is Ms Liew."

Brian found a place at one of the low tables and sat down with his hands in his lap, the way he thought a proper schoolboy should. Ms Liew clapped her hands softly for quiet.

"Now, boys and girls," she said, "because it is your very first day, we are going to start with something nice and easy. We are going to practise resting. I want everyone to fold your arms on the table, put your head down, close your eyes, and pretend to be asleep. Can you do that for me?"

Brian could do that. He folded his arms, laid his cheek against them, and shut his eyes. The table was cool and smooth under his face. Somewhere far off Ms Liew was still talking, her voice soft and warm, and the morning sun came through the window and lay across his back like a blanket. This is easy, he thought. I am very good at pretending to sleep.

And then, without meaning to at all, he fell properly asleep.

He did not hear Ms Liew clap her hands again. He did not hear her say, "Wake up now, everyone, it is time for our next game!" He did not hear the scrape of twenty small chairs as the other children lifted their heads and sat up. Brian slept on, deeply and happily, his mouth a little open, while the whole class moved along without him.

Out in the corridor, Mum and Dad watched their son lie motionless at his table while every other child got up. Mum's hand went to the glass. Dad knocked on the window, harder than he meant to.

"Teacher," he called through the pane. "Is he all right? Why isn't he getting up?"

Ms Liew came to the window, glanced back at Brian, and laughed.

"Oh, please don't worry," she said. "Your son is simply a very good sleeper. He is perfectly fine."

When Brian finally woke, the light had moved across the table and the other children were already crayoning at the far end of the room. For a moment he could not think where he was. His cheek was warm, and there was a small damp patch on his arm where he had slept.

He looked up, and there at the window were Mum and Dad, still watching, their worried faces breaking now into relief. Brian could not understand why they had looked so frightened. He had only had the nicest sleep of his life.

Ms Liew crouched down beside him and smiled, as though boys fell fast asleep on their very first morning every day of the week.

"Welcome to kindergarten, Brian," she said softly. "Shall we go and draw?"
