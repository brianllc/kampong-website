Upper Primary · ~1992

A Hidden Find

In those days, almost every boy Brian knew was mad about Dragon Ball, and it was not enough just to read the comics. You had to collect the cards too.

The cards came from a row of machines outside the mamak shop at the back of the school. The shop stood just beyond the back gate, on the way to the bus stop, so that Brian passed it every single day on his walk home, and every single day the machines stood there waiting for him. Each one was a tall metal box with a little window at the front, and behind the glass was a picture from the Dragon Ball comic series. It cost twenty cents a go. You dropped your coin into the slot, turned the dial with a heavy clunk, and a single card came sliding out of the mouth at the bottom.

Most of the time it was an ordinary card, Wukong or Krillin or Piccolo printed flat on plain paper. But every so often, if you were lucky, out came a shiny: a rare "gold" card that caught the light and threw it back in little rainbows, the kind every boy wanted and very few had.

There was a secret to the machines, and all the boys knew it. Just before a shiny appeared, the machine would push out two cards at once instead of one. That was the sign. So whenever a boy turned the dial and two cards dropped together, the others would crowd round at once.

"Two cards! Two cards!" they would shout. "Next one shiny already!"

And the next twenty cents would go in, with everybody holding their breath.

It was a wonderful feeling and sometimes a terrible one, because the sign did not care whether you had money left. Once, Brian watched a boy put in his very last coin and get the double, then stand there with empty pockets as the next boy in line stepped up, dropped in his twenty cents, and took the shiny that should have been his.

One afternoon, Brian turned the dial and got a card that was not like the others at all.

It was not shiny. But when he ran his thumb across it, the surface was smooth, smooth in a way no paper card had ever felt. It was almost like plastic. He stood turning it over and over, puzzled.

Ivan saw it and his eyes went wide.

"Eh, what card is that? Let me see." He took it, rubbed it the way Brian had, and looked up. "So smooth. I never see this kind before. I buy from you, can? I give you fifty cents."

Fifty cents was more than two whole goes on the machine. Brian almost said yes. He had his hand half out.

But there was something about the card he did not want to let go of, some small itch of curiosity, and at the last moment he closed his fingers round it.

"Wait," said Brian. "Let me see something first."

The smooth surface, he had noticed, was not quite stuck down at one corner. Very carefully, with the edge of his nail, he began to peel it back. Ivan leaned in close. The smooth layer lifted away, slow and clean, like the skin off a longan.

And underneath, hidden all this time beneath the dull cover, was a shiny.

It was a gold Wukong, his staff swung high over his head, throwing the afternoon light into little rainbows across both boys' faces.

For a moment neither of them said anything at all. It was the first hidden shiny either of them had ever found, and they bent over it together as if it might disappear.
