The Day My Little Sister Was Born

"We're having a baby!" Mum and Dad announced one morning.

Brian and his big sister Annie jumped up and down. They could not wait to have someone new to play with.

"I hope it's a boy," Brian said. "Then we can play trucks and soldiers together."

"No way!" Annie protested. "I want a baby sister. I'm sick of having a brother."

They argued back and forth, boy, girl, boy, girl, until Dad held up both hands.

"Now, now, let's not argue," he said. "We won't know until the baby comes, anyway."

Then Brian thought of something that changed his mind completely. "Wait. If it's a boy, he'll have to share my room, won't he?"

Dad nodded. "Yes, that's right."

Suddenly Brian did not want a baby brother at all. He wanted a baby sister too. It was remarkable how quickly he changed his mind once his own bedroom was at stake, though that was too plain a thing for Brian to notice about himself just then.

One night, Brian was jolted awake by Dad's voice in the dark. "Come on, quickly. Get dressed. We need to go."

Brian rubbed his eyes and squinted at the clock. It was still black outside the window, and most of the lights at the opposite HDB block were off. "Huh? Why are we going to school so early?" he mumbled.

Mum's voice came from the other room, tight and a little breathless. "I think the baby's coming!"

Brian's heart began to race. He threw on whatever clothes he could find as quickly as he could. They all piled into a taxi downstairs, and the driver sped off through the empty streets towards KK Hospital.

When they arrived, Dad pressed a big note into the driver's hand. "Thank you for driving us there safely and quickly," he said.

Then came the waiting. Brian and Annie sat in the waiting room and tried to keep awake. Brian had woken far too early, and the chairs were warm, and his eyes kept closing.

He was half asleep when the nurse came out of the delivery room and said: "It's a girl!"

Brian blinked and rubbed his eyes. "A girl?" he repeated, hardly believing it.

The nurse then led them down a long hallway to Mum's ward. Mum was lying in bed, propped up against the pillows, tired but smiling, with a small bundle held close in her arms. Brian crept up to the side of the bed and peered in. There was his baby sister, tiny and wrinkled and pink, with a single soft tuft of hair on the top of her head.

"Wow," Brian breathed. "I'm a big brother now."

Annie looked too, and smiled. "She's so cute," she said.

"Her name is Cherie," Mum said. She looked at the three of them together for the first time. "Annie, Brian and Cherie. That way you are the ABC siblings."

Brian thought that was very easy to remember.

They spent the whole morning at the hospital, taking turns to hold the baby and gaze at her. When it was finally time to go home, Brian felt a little sad to leave his mum and new sister behind. Then he remembered something, and a grin spread across his face.

"I got to skip school today!"

Annie rolled her eyes. Brian did not care one bit.
