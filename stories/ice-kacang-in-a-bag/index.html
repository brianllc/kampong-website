Ice Kacang in a Bag

<!-- status: awaiting read · level: P5 · year: 1993 -->

The bus home stopped at the bottom of the hill, which was the wrong end of it.

Brian's block stood at the top. Between the bus stop and his own front door there were flights and flights of stairs, and by the time school let out the sun had been standing on them all day. He would come down off the bus, the heat would come up off the pavement to meet him, and he would look up the hill and know exactly how long it was going to take.

But one flight up from the bus stop was Block 26. The bookshop where he bought his comics was at one end of it, and the kopitiam at the other. On the hot days, if there was any money left in his pocket by then, Brian walked the length of the block to the far end.

"Uncle, ice kacang," he said. "To take away, please."

"Ice kacang dabao," the uncle said. "Fifty cents."

Brian put the coin on the counter and watched. The uncle clamped a block of ice into the machine and wound the handle, and the ice came down in a soft white heap, and he caught the heap in a clear plastic drink bag held open in one hand. Then everything went in on top of it, straight into the bag: attap chee, red beans, a spoon of sweet corn, then the syrups in a red stripe and a green one, then evaporated milk poured over the whole lot in a slow white curl. He stood a straw in it and handed it over with the top still open.

Then Brian continued his journey up the hill.

There was no spoon and no bowl, so he ate it walking, squeezing the bag from the bottom to push the ice and the beans and the corn up to the mouth where he could get at them. The straw was for later, for the melted ice at the bottom. His hand went numb and pink from the cold. It was the best thing in the world on a hot afternoon and he ate it far too fast, giving him a brain freeze.

On days like this, the summer wasn't so hot and the climb wasn't so long.
