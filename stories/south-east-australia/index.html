Upper Primary · 1993

South East Australia

In 1993, the whole of Singapore was watching the SEA Games, because that year the Games had come to Singapore.

Every evening on the television there were swimmers and sprinters and weightlifters, and every morning the newspapers were full of who had won which medal. At school it was all anyone talked about. So nobody was very surprised when, at Monday assembly, Mr Tan stepped up to the microphone at the front of the school hall and announced that there would be a quiz.

"Boys and girls," said Mr Tan, his voice crackling out across the rows of children, "this week, in honour of the SEA Games, we are going to have a little quiz at assembly. If you think you know the answer to a question, put up your hand. If I call you up and you are right, you win a prize."

A ripple ran through the whole school. A prize.

The first question was an easy one.

"Which country are this year's SEA Games being held in?"

Half the hall put up a hand. A tall girl from Primary Six was called out, said "Singapore" into the microphone, and went back to her place clutching a brand new packet of coloured pencils. Four hundred children watched her go and burned with envy.

Brian did not put his hand up, and it was not because he did not know. It was because it was the afternoon session, and assembly came straight after lunch, and the hall was warm and full of standing children, and he had arrived at the part of the day where his eyes were open and very little else was. Mr Tan's voice came crackling out of the speakers the way a radio comes through a wall in another flat. Brian heard it without listening to it at all.

Then Mr Tan said, "Here is a harder one. Can anybody tell me what the letters S, E, A stand for? What does SEA stand for?"

Silence. Four hundred children looked at their shoes. Even the big Primary Six pupils said nothing at all.

Mr Tan waited. Then he smiled and gave a clue. "Let me help you. S stands for South and E stands for East. South-East, and then what does A stand for?"

And something went click in Brian's head, and he woke up.

On the wall of his classroom there hung a large map of the world, and Brian had spent many a long, boring afternoon staring at it. He knew it well. He knew Singapore, a tiny red dot near the middle. And he knew that if you went down and to the right of Singapore, down towards the south and the east, you came at last to a great big country sitting all by itself at the bottom of the map.

South-east. Of course. Brian's hand shot up before he could think twice, which was the trouble, because thinking twice was precisely what he needed.

"Yes! You there, with your hand up," said Mr Tan, pointing to Brian.

Brian walked out from his row and across the front of the entire school, four hundred faces turning to follow him, and he was wide awake now and not nervous in the least, because he was certain. He took the microphone the way he had seen the others do it, and he said, loudly and clearly, so that even the children right at the back could hear him:

"The answer is Australia!"

Mr Tan shook his head.

"Not quite," he said, not unkindly. "Good try, though. Let's have someone else." And he looked out across the rows for another raised hand.

Brian handed back the microphone and walked back to his place. He did not get the coloured pencils, and worse than that, he could not for the life of him work out what he had got wrong.

He thought about it for the whole rest of assembly. The letters were S, E, A. Australia was south-east of Singapore. He had seen it on the map a hundred times, the big country down and to the right, sitting all on its own. And it began with an A. It was the only country to the south-east of Singapore that began with an A. Where on earth had he gone wrong?

Author's note: SEA stands for South East Asia, which is the part of the world Singapore sits in, together with Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines and their neighbours. The SEA Games are held every two years for the countries of that region, and Singapore hosted them in 1993. So the A was never a country at all. Australia, for its part, is not in South East Asia, and has never been invited.
